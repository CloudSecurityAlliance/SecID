## 2. Resolution of the Five Design Questions in `SecID#208`

### Question 1: Lifecycle status for bills (`proposed`, `enacted`, `in-force`, `expired`/`died`, with `status_as_of` date)
- **Current Registry Solution (No Schema Break):** Every entry added or updated in this batch carries an explicit structured prefix in `data.notes`: `Status: <Proposed | Enacted | In force | Died on Order Paper> (<effective/stage details>; verified as of 2026-11-03).`
- **Schema Evolution Note:** `versions_available[].status` currently uses the enum `["current", "supported", "deprecated", "superseded", "draft", "withdrawn"]`, which models *document edition* lifecycle rather than *legislative enactment* state. If structured machine-filterable legislative status is needed across `regulation`, an optional `legal_status` object (`{"state": "proposed"|"enacted"|"in_force"|"repealed"|"expired", "as_of": "YYYY-MM-DD", "effective_date": "YYYY-MM-DD"}`) on `match_nodes[].data` is the clean additive path without overloading `versions_available`.

### Question 2: Bill-to-Act transition (what happens when a registered bill is enacted?)
- **Rule Applied:** Because `match_nodes[].patterns` is an ordered OR-list where `patterns[0]` defines the canonical slug (enforced by `scripts/check-canonical-names.py`), SecID handles Bill-to-Act transitions without breaking historical citations:
  1. When a bill has a stable short title or codified citation (e.g., Illinois `wopr-act`, New York `ai-companion-models-law`), make the Act/codified slug `patterns[0]` and keep the bill number and Public Act number (`hb-1806`, `pa-104-0054`, `s3008c-part-u`, `gbl-art-47`) as secondary patterns on the **same** `match_node`.
  2. When a jurisdiction cites enacted statutes primarily by their bill number (as in California `sb-243`, `sb-1119`, `sb-867` or Utah `hb-452`), `sb-243` remains `patterns[0]` before and after enactment, with the codified chapter/section (`Cal. Bus. & Prof. Code § 22600 et seq.`) recorded in `alternate_names` and secondary patterns.
  3. When a pending bill registered by bill number (e.g., `bill-c-34`, `guard-act-s3062`) is later enacted under a final Act citation, both the bill pattern and the Act pattern remain on the same node so existing `secid:regulation/...` references never rot.
  4. When an enacted statute is subsequently regulated by a separate executive or presidential decree with its own official legal text URL (e.g., Brazil's *Lei nº 15.211/2025* and *Decreto nº 12.880/2026*), each legal instrument gets its own `match_node` (`eca-digital` and `decreto-12880-2026`) so each resolves directly to its own official legal text.

### Question 3: Namespaces for US federal/state bills and UK Acts/Bills
- **Follow Existing Registry Convention:** Rather than fragmenting namespaces between bill trackers and statute consolidators (e.g., splitting `parl.ca` vs `justice.gc.ca`, or `gov.uk` vs `legislation.gov.uk`, or `ca.gov` vs `leginfo.legislature.ca.gov`), SecID already established single jurisdiction namespaces under `registry/regulation/`:
  - US Federal: `congress.gov` (`registry/regulation/gov/congress.json`)
  - US States: `ca.gov`, `ny.gov`, `utah.gov`, `illinois.gov`, `oregon.gov`, `wa.gov`, `nebraska.gov`
  - UK: `gov.uk` (`registry/regulation/uk/gov.json`), with `urls` pointing directly to `https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/...` for enacted Acts and `gov.uk`/`bills.parliament.uk` for bills.
  - Canada: `parl.ca` (`registry/regulation/ca/parl.json`), with `urls` pointing to both `parl.ca/legisinfo` and `laws-lois.justice.gc.ca`.
- Keeping bills and acts in the same jurisdiction namespace guarantees that a bill does not have to change its *namespace* when it receives Royal Assent or presidential/gubernatorial signature.

### Question 4: Type for Australia's eSafety Phase 2 Industry Codes (`regulation` vs `control`)
- **Decision:** Registered under **`regulation`** (`secid:regulation/esafety.gov.au/phase-2-industry-codes`).
- **Rationale:** Under Part 9 of the *Online Safety Act 2021* (Cth), once an Industry Code is registered by the eSafety Commissioner, compliance is mandatory and legally enforceable by the regulator via formal directions, enforceable undertakings, injunctions, and civil penalty orders up to 50,000 penalty units (~AU$49.5 million). Per `CLASSIFY.md`, legally binding and government-enforced regulatory instruments belong in `regulation`.

### Question 5: Finding the set as a set (Topic Tag vs Relationship Layer)
- **Decision:** Keep `tags` focused on stable geographic/jurisdictional axes (`country`, `region`) and maintain thematic cross-jurisdiction groupings (like "laws and bills regulating AI chatbots and AI companions") in curated research/mapping inventories (this document and the Relationship Layer roadmap) rather than proliferating ad-hoc topical tags (`topic: ["ai-chatbot"]`) across namespace schemas.
