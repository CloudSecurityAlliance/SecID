## 1. Scope & Classification Rules Applied

Per [`docs/guides/CLASSIFY.md`](../guides/CLASSIFY.md) and [`docs/reference/TYPES-AND-SUBTYPES.md`](../reference/TYPES-AND-SUBTYPES.md), national security strategy materials span three SecID types depending on their legal character and structural addressability:

1. **`reference` (with `"subtype": ["strategy"]`)** — Whole-of-government national security strategies, national cyber security strategies, defence policy updates, multi-year action/implementation plans, annual implementation/progress reports, mid-term reviews, horizontal program evaluations, public consultation green papers, and "What We Heard / What We Learned" synthesis reports that set policy direction or audit strategy execution without themselves constituting binding statutes or enumerable control catalogues.
2. **`regulation`** — Enacted statutes, presidential decrees, or parliamentary acts that either:
   - enact a national security/cybersecurity strategy directly into binding law (e.g., Brazil's *Decreto nº 10.222/2020* enacting E-Ciber), or
   - mandate the statutory review cycle, oversight authorities, and parliamentary reporting processes for national security policy (e.g., Canada's *National Security Act, 2017*, *NSIRA Act*, *NSICOP Act*, and *Critical Cyber Systems Protection Act*; Australia's *Cyber Security Act 2024*; Japan's *Basic Act on Cybersecurity* and *Active Cyber Defense Act*).
3. **`control`** — National cybersecurity frameworks, baseline catalogues, or compliance standards issued alongside a national strategy that contain individually addressable control requirements (e.g., UAE *National Information Assurance Standard* `secid:control/csc.gov.ae/ias`, Qatar *National Information Assurance Standard* `secid:control/ncsa.gov.qa/nia`).

**Multi-edition resolution note:** For multi-edition `reference` entries (such as `national-security-strategy` or `national-cyber-security-strategy`), querying with `#<edition>` (e.g., `secid:reference/whitehouse.gov/national-security-strategy#2022` or `@2022#2022`) resolves directly to that specific edition's primary-source URL via the version child node (`ResolutionResult`), whereas querying at the source level with `@<edition>` alone (`secid:reference/whitehouse.gov/national-security-strategy@2022`) returns the source-level `RegistryResult` containing the structured per-edition `versions_available` array.

## 2. SecID Taxonomy & Modeling Notes for This Region

1. **Strategies Promulgated as Binding Presidential/Prime Ministerial Decrees (`regulation` + `strategy` cross-reference):**
   - **Indonesia (`ID`):** *Perpres No. 47 Tahun 2023* (NCSS) and *Peraturan BSSN No. 5 Tahun 2025* (National Cybersecurity Action Plan 2024–2028) are binding statutory instruments in `peraturan.go.id` / `peraturan.bpk.go.id`.
   - **Vietnam (`VN`):** Both the National Cybersecurity Strategy (*Decision No. 964/QĐ-TTg*) and National AI Strategy (*Decision No. 127/QĐ-TTg*) are Prime Ministerial Decisions under `chinhphu.vn` (which already hosts `secid:regulation/chinhphu.vn/...`).
   - **Philippines (`PH`):** Both *NSP 2023–2028* (*EO No. 37, s. 2023*) and *NCSP 2023–2028* (*EO No. 58, s. 2024*) are formally adopted by Presidential Executive Order.
   - **Central Asia (`KZ`, `UZ`, `KG`, `TJ`, `TM`):** Almost all national security concepts, cybersecurity strategies (*Cyber Shield*, *Digital Uzbekistan 2030*), and AI strategies (*KZ Resolution No. 592*, *UZ PQ-358*, *TJ Resolution No. 489*) are enacted via Presidential Decrees or Government Resolutions published in official legal databases (`adilet.zan.kz`, `lex.uz`, `cbd.minjust.gov.kg`, `mmk.tj`).
2. **Pacific Island Regional Pattern:**
   - Five Pacific Island nations have formal standalone National Security Strategies/Policies: **Papua New Guinea (`PG`, `@2013` & `@2024-2029`)**, **Vanuatu (`VU`, `@2019`)**, **Samoa (`WS`, `@2018`)**, **Solomon Islands (`SB`, `@2020` & `@2025-2028`)**, and **Palau (`PW`, `@2022-2026`)**.
   - Seven Pacific Island nations have published National Cybersecurity Strategies/Frameworks/Roadmaps: **Papua New Guinea (`PG`)**, **Fiji (`FJ`)**, **Vanuatu (`VU`)**, **Samoa (`WS`)**, **Tonga (`TO`)**, **Micronesia (`FM`)**, and **Kiribati (`KI`)** (while **Palau (`PW`)** embeds cybersecurity into its NSS and **Solomon Islands (`SB`)** covers it in its SIG ICT & Digital Strategy).
   - **Papua New Guinea (`PG`)** is the first Pacific Island state to publish a dedicated *National Sovereign AI & Blockchain Strategy* (`@2025`), while **Fiji (`FJ`)** and **Samoa (`WS`)** address AI within their 2025–2030 National Digital/ICT Strategies.
